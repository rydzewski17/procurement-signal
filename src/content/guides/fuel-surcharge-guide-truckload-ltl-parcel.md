## Why fuel surcharges matter more now

Diesel set a record above $6.50 a gallon in September 2026 and was about $6.18 in mid-October, according to EIA data reported by FreightWaves. With fuel that high, the surcharge can be a third or more of the cost of a truckload, and small differences in how it's calculated add up quickly. See our [diesel outlook](/articles/2026-10-14-diesel-record-russia-deal-red-dye-order-fuel-surcharges/).

## How truckload fuel surcharges work

Most truckload contracts charge fuel per mile using a simple formula:

**Fuel surcharge per mile = (current diesel price − base price) ÷ miles per gallon**

- **Current diesel price:** usually the EIA weekly U.S. on-highway diesel average, national or regional, published each Monday.
- **Base price ("peg"):** the diesel price assumed to be included in the linehaul rate. Below it, no surcharge applies.
- **Miles per gallon:** the fuel economy the formula assumes, often between 5 and 7.

### Worked examples

Using a $1.25 base and 6.5 mpg:

| Weekly diesel price | Surcharge per mile | On a 500-mile load | On a 1,000-mile load |
|---|---|---|---|
| $4.00 | $0.42 | $212 | $423 |
| $5.00 | $0.58 | $288 | $577 |
| $5.50 | $0.65 | $327 | $654 |
| $6.18 | $0.76 | $379 | $758 |
| $6.50 | $0.81 | $404 | $808 |

Every 10-cent change in diesel moves the surcharge by about 1.5 cents a mile under these assumptions. These are illustrative calculations; your contract terms decide your numbers.

### Why base price and mpg matter

Two carriers can quote the same linehaul rate but very different total costs:

| Carrier | Base | MPG | Surcharge at $6.18 | Total on 1,000 miles with $2.50 linehaul |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carrier A | $1.25 | 6.5 | $0.76/mi | $3,258 |
| Carrier B | $2.00 | 6.0 | $0.70/mi | $3,197 |
| Carrier C | $1.00 | 5.5 | $0.94/mi | $3,442 |

Illustrative calculations. Always compare total cost, not linehaul alone.

## LTL and parcel fuel surcharges

**LTL** carriers usually charge fuel as a percentage of linehaul charges, from a published table indexed to the weekly EIA diesel price. Because it's a percentage, a general rate increase also raises the dollar value of the fuel surcharge.

**Parcel** carriers publish weekly fuel surcharge percentages for ground and air services, applied to transportation charges and some surcharges. Tables change periodically, and changes are often as significant as the annual rate increase.

## What to put in carrier contracts

1. **Name the index.** Specify the EIA series (national or regional) and the day it's read.
2. **Fix the base and mpg** for the contract term, and use the same assumptions for every carrier in a bid.
3. **Set the timing.** State which week's price applies to a shipment: pickup date or invoice date.
4. **Cap percentage surcharges** for LTL and parcel, or negotiate a discount off the published table.
5. **Allow audits.** Reserve the right to audit fuel charges and recover overbilling.
6. **Review quarterly.** Agree to revisit the table if fuel moves outside a set range.

## How to audit fuel surcharges

- Pull invoice-level fuel charges and miles.
- Recalculate the expected surcharge using the contract formula and the correct week's EIA price.
- Flag variances above a threshold, such as 1%.
- Recover overcharges monthly; small per-load errors add up over thousands of loads.
