## What supplier risk management is

Supplier risk management (SRM) is how procurement teams make sure suppliers keep delivering the right goods, at the agreed price and standard, on time. It is a continuous process, not a one-time onboarding check, because the risks change: a supplier's finances weaken, a tariff changes, a port closes, a regulation takes effect.

In 2026 the stakes are high. New tariffs tie cost to [where suppliers are located](/guides/us-tariffs-explained-procurement/), forced-labor rules make tier-2 visibility a compliance requirement, and input prices are moving sharply in different directions.

## Step 1: Segment your supply base

You can't monitor every supplier deeply. Segment them on two axes:

| | Easy to replace | Hard to replace |
|---|---|---|
| **High spend** | Leverage: compete regularly | **Strategic: monitor continuously** |
| **Low spend** | Routine: basic checks only | **Bottleneck: secure alternatives** |

Strategic and bottleneck suppliers are typically 10–20% of the supply base, and they carry most of the risk.

## Step 2: Score risk on five dimensions

Score each priority supplier from 1 (low) to 5 (high) on each dimension:

1. **Financial.** Credit ratings, payment behavior, profitability, debt load and dependence on your business. A supplier for whom you are over 30% of revenue is fragile in both directions.
2. **Geopolitical and trade.** Country of origin, tariff exposure, sanctions, export controls and political stability. Include the supplier's own sources.
3. **Operational.** Capacity, quality record, single-site production, natural-hazard exposure and logistics routes.
4. **Compliance and ESG.** Forced-labor exposure (including UFLPA and the 2026 forced-labor tariffs), environmental rules, anti-corruption and product safety.
5. **Cyber.** Access to your systems or data, security certifications and incident history.

Multiply each score by **impact**: what happens to you if this supplier fails? Probability × impact gives a priority ranking.

## Step 3: Map beyond tier 1

Ask strategic suppliers to disclose their key sub-suppliers and manufacturing locations for critical components and raw materials. Prioritize:

- Components with a single source anywhere in the chain.
- Raw materials from regions with forced-labor or sanctions exposure.
- Sub-suppliers concentrated in one geography or port.

## Step 4: Monitor continuously

Set up signals that tell you when a score should change:

- **Financial:** credit-rating changes, late payments, layoffs, bankruptcy filings.
- **Trade:** tariff actions and deadlines. Our [Tariff & Trade Deadline Tracker](/trackers/tariff-deadlines/) lists them.
- **Cost:** sharp moves in the supplier's input costs, which raise the chance of price demands or distress. See the [Cost Pressure Monitor](/cost-monitor/).
- **Operational:** delivery performance, quality escapes, news of disruptions.
- **Compliance:** enforcement actions, detentions, audit findings.

Review strategic suppliers at least quarterly, and re-score immediately when a signal fires.

## Step 5: Reduce the risks that matter

| Risk | Mitigation options |
|---|---|
| Single source | Qualify a second source; dual-award volume |
| Supplier financial distress | Shorter payment terms for them, supply-chain financing, tooling ownership |
| Tariff and origin exposure | Alternative-origin sourcing, USMCA qualification, contract tariff clauses |
| Price volatility | Index-linked pricing, caps and floors, forward buys |
| Disruption | Safety stock for critical parts, alternative logistics routes |
| Forced-labor exposure | Traceability documentation, audits, supplier codes of conduct |

## Step 6: Govern and report

Assign an owner for each strategic supplier, keep risk scores in one place, and report the top risks and actions to leadership each quarter. A short, regular report beats a large, stale database.
