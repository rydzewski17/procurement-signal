## The market in 2026

Ocean freight split by trade lane in 2026. In early October, Asia–U.S. West Coast spot rates held above $8,000 per forty-foot unit while Asia–North Europe rates were down 34% since July 1, according to Xeneta data reported by FreightWaves. Global schedule reliability fell to 27% in September, with an average delay of six days. See our coverage of [rates](/articles/2026-10-14-ocean-rates-split-transpacific-high-asia-europe-falls-oocl/) and [reliability](/articles/2026-10-14-ocean-schedule-reliability-27-percent-vessel-delays-six-days/).

## Contract types

| Type | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed annual rate | One rate per lane for the contract term | Stable volumes, budgeting certainty |
| Index-linked | Rate moves with a published index, often with floors and ceilings | Volatile markets, sharing upside and downside |
| Short-term or quarterly | Rates reset each quarter | Markets expected to fall |
| Spot | Priced per shipment | Overflow and irregular volume |

A common approach is a blend: fixed rates on a core share and index-linked or short-term pricing on the rest.

## What to negotiate besides the rate

**Allocation.** A contract rate is worth little if the carrier won't load your boxes. Specify weekly allocation by lane, and what happens when you under- or over-ship.

**Blank sailing protection.** Require notice periods for cancelled sailings and guaranteed space on the next sailing.

**Named services.** Identify the service strings and port rotations you depend on, with notice if they change.

**Surcharges.** Separate the base rate from bunker, peak season, congestion and canal surcharges. Define how each is calculated and when it can apply.

**Reliability.** Ask for monthly on-time performance by service and agree remedies, such as credits or volume flexibility, if it falls below a set level.

**Free time.** Negotiate demurrage and detention free time, which can cost more than the rate difference between bids.

## Running an ocean tender

1. **Build a lane file** with origin and destination ports, annual and monthly volumes by container type, and seasonality.
2. **Invite carriers and forwarders.** Carriers offer direct contracts; forwarders add flexibility, consolidation and service across carriers.
3. **Request structured bids** with base rates, surcharges, allocation, free time and reliability commitments.
4. **Compare total landed cost**, including expected demurrage and the inventory cost of delays.
5. **Award primary and backup** on key lanes.

## The cost of unreliability

For a company importing $10 million of goods a month, an extra six days in transit ties up roughly $2 million more in inventory, and at a 10% annual carrying cost that's about $200,000 a year, by our calculation. Reliability terms are worth negotiating.

## When to negotiate

| Trade lane | Typical contract season |
|---|---|
| Trans-Pacific (Asia–U.S.) | Early spring, for contracts starting around May |
| Asia–Europe | Fourth quarter, for calendar-year contracts |
| Trans-Atlantic | Varies; often aligned with calendar year |
