## The 2027 market in brief

Truckload contract rates hit a 52-week high in October 2026, and analysts describe the repricing as only about halfway done because carriers are leaving the market faster than freight is falling. That changes how a bid should be run: the goal is capacity you can rely on at a fair price, not the lowest number on a spreadsheet. See our [market analysis](/articles/2026-10-14-truckload-contract-rates-52-week-high-bid-season/).

## Step 1: Build the lane file

Pull at least 12 months of shipment data and summarize by lane:

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Origin and destination (city, state, ZIP) | Defines the lane carriers will price |
| Annual and weekly volume | Lets carriers size capacity |
| Seasonality | Peaks drive rejections |
| Equipment (dry van, reefer, flatbed) | Different markets and rates |
| Appointment and dwell times | Carriers price slow facilities higher |
| Special requirements | Hazmat, team service, drop trailers |

Clean data is the single biggest lever. Carriers price uncertainty into rates.

## Step 2: Choose carriers to invite

- **Incumbents** with good performance on your lanes.
- **Asset carriers** with terminals or density near your origins.
- **Brokers** for coverage on irregular or remote lanes.
- **Verify every new carrier** for authority, insurance and identity; [freight fraud](/articles/2026-10-14-freight-fraud-fake-broker-cargo-theft-verification/) is rising.

## Step 3: Set the rules

- **Price basis:** linehaul per mile or flat rate per load, excluding fuel.
- **Fuel:** a fixed surcharge table indexed to the weekly EIA diesel price, the same for every bidder. See our [fuel surcharge guide](/guides/fuel-surcharge-guide-truckload-ltl-parcel/).
- **Accessorials:** a standard schedule for detention, layover, stop-offs and driver assist.
- **Term:** 12 months with a mid-year review for core lanes; shorter for volatile ones.
- **Volume commitments:** what you'll tender and what carriers commit to accept.

## Step 4: Analyze bids

Look beyond the lowest rate:

| Factor | What to check |
|---|---|
| Rate vs. market | Compare with current contract benchmarks; rates far below market are likely to be rejected |
| Carrier capacity | Does the carrier actually run near this lane? |
| Past performance | Tender acceptance and on-time history |
| Concentration | Avoid too much volume with one carrier or broker |

## Step 5: Award

| Lane type | Strategy |
|---|---|
| Core, high-volume, steady | Primary and backup carriers, 12-month award |
| Medium volume, seasonal | Two or three carriers, six-month award |
| Low volume, irregular | Broker or managed spot with a ceiling |
| Refrigerated | Fewer, committed carriers; expect bigger increases |

## Step 6: Implement and measure

- Load awards into your TMS routing guide promptly.
- Track monthly: tender acceptance by carrier and lane, on-time pickup and delivery, invoice accuracy and total cost per mile including fuel and accessorials.
- Re-bid or re-award lanes where acceptance falls below your threshold.

## Common mistakes

1. **Awarding the lowest rate in a rising market.** It looks good on paper and fails in practice.
2. **Letting each carrier bid its own fuel table.** You can't compare bids.
3. **Ignoring facility performance.** Long dwell times cost you rejections and detention.
4. **No backup carriers.** When the primary rejects, the spot market sets your price.
