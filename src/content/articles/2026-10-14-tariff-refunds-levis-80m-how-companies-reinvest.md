## Why it matters

Refunds of the tariffs the Supreme Court invalidated are now material line items. Levi's alone recorded about $80 million. For procurement, two questions follow: are you capturing every refund your company is owed, and did suppliers who passed those tariffs through to you owe you a share back?

## The details

Levi Strauss & Co. recorded roughly $80 million in tariff refunds in its most recent quarter, CFO Harmit Singh said on the company's Oct. 7 earnings call. The refunds stem from the Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling that nullified the administration's country-specific duties.

Levi's plans to reinvest about three-quarters, roughly $60 million:

- About a third, roughly $20 million, goes to distribution and logistics
- The rest is split between marketing and promotions, with a small portion for inventory clearance before the holidays
- About $25 million was invested in the quarter that ended Aug. 30, and about $35 million is planned for the current quarter

Net tariff recovery is expected to add about 80 basis points to Levi's projected gross margin of roughly 63% for the year ending Nov. 29.

Other companies are using refunds differently. McCormick received about $28 million and Kimberly-Clark about $45 million, both to offset higher input costs. E.l.f. Beauty expects about $58.5 million and plans to fund price cuts.

## What procurement teams should do

- **Inventory your refund exposure.** Work with trade compliance to list every entry that paid the invalidated duties, by supplier and HTS code.
- **Check supplier pass-throughs.** If suppliers raised prices citing those tariffs, review contract language and request credits or price adjustments where duties are refunded to them.
- **Influence the reinvestment.** Make the case for funding resilience projects, such as dual sourcing, inventory buffers or logistics upgrades, as Levi's did with distribution.
- **Keep landed-cost models current.** Remove refunded duties from forward cost models, but keep scenarios for the tariffs still in force under other authorities. See our [tariffs explainer](/guides/us-tariffs-explained-procurement/).

## What to watch next

- **More refund disclosures.** Expect more companies to report refunds in Q3 and Q4 earnings; ask your suppliers whether they received any.
- **Supplier pricing.** Where suppliers recovered duties they had passed through, there is a case for price credits.
- **Replacement tariffs.** Section 301 and Section 232 actions have replaced much of the struck-down duty, so refunds are not a sign that costs are falling.

## The bigger picture

Refunds are a one-time cash event layered on top of tariffs that remain under Section 232 and Section 301. Companies are using them for margin, pricing or supply chain spend. Procurement teams that come with a prioritized list of supplier and logistics projects are best placed to direct that money.
