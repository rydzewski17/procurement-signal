## Why it matters

The Federal Acquisition Regulation decides how the U.S. government buys almost everything. The rewrite now underway changes the rules companies must follow to sell to federal agencies and the processes agency procurement teams use. The second set of proposed rules covers some of the most heavily used parts, including commercial products and services, simplified acquisitions, negotiated competition and contract types.

## The details

The Revolutionary FAR Overhaul entered formal notice-and-comment rulemaking on June 23, 2026, with a first set of proposed rules covering 20 FAR parts and associated clauses, according to law firm Wiley. A second set followed on Sept. 18, covering 16 more parts. Each set has a 30-day public comment period.

**FAR parts in the second set:**

| Part | Subject |
|---|---|
| 8 | Required sources of supplies and services |
| 9 | Contractor qualifications |
| 12 | Acquisition of commercial products and services |
| 13 | Simplified acquisition procedures |
| 14 | Sealed bidding |
| 15 | Contracting by negotiation |
| 16 | Types of contracts |
| 17 | Special contracting methods |
| 27 | Patents, data and copyrights |
| 28 | Bonds and insurance |
| 35 | Research and development contracting |
| 36 | Construction and architect-engineer contracts |
| 38 | Federal supply schedule contracting |
| 44 | Subcontracting policies and procedures |
| 47 | Transportation |
| 51 | Use of government sources by contractors |

Part titles are the standard FAR part names.

Wiley notes that the second-round proposals go further than the interim class deviation text agencies have been using, and some fold in other pending rulemakings. Related actions this summer included a proposed consolidated security and supply chain framework (Part 40), a revised framework for controlled unclassified information, and updated Part 16 deviation text implementing an executive order on preferring fixed-price contracts.

A final set of four notices covering the remaining FAR parts is expected in 2026. Those will also be proposals; no final rules have been issued yet.

## Who should pay attention

| You are... | Focus on | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A commercial supplier selling to agencies | Parts 12, 13, 38 | Commercial terms, simplified buys and schedules |
| A services or construction contractor | Parts 15, 16, 36 | Competition rules and contract types, including fixed-price preference |
| A prime contractor | Parts 9, 44 | Responsibility determinations and subcontracting rules |
| An agency procurement team | All, especially 8, 13, 15, 16 | How you run acquisitions day to day |

## What procurement teams should do

- **Read the proposals that touch your contracts** and comment during the 30-day window if a change would affect you.
- **Map current contracts to new rules.** Note which clauses are likely to change at the next modification or option.
- **Watch fixed-price preference.** If you sell services under cost-type contracts, prepare for more fixed-price structures.
- **Track the security framework.** The proposed Part 40 consolidates supply chain and security requirements that affect suppliers.

## What to watch next

- **The final set of proposed rules** later in 2026.
- **Final rules** after comments are reviewed.
- **Agency deviations** that apply changes before final rules.

## The bigger picture

The overhaul is meant to simplify federal buying and make it easier for commercial companies to sell to the government, the same goal behind the Pentagon's move away from Cost Accounting Standards described in our [defense supplier coverage](/articles/2026-10-14-raytheon-30-7b-multiyear-missile-contracts-defense-supplier-ramp/).
