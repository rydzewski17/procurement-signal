## Why it matters

Freight is one of the few categories where procurement can feel a market turn within a single bid cycle. Contract truckload rates are rising for the 21st straight month on Cass's index, and the drivers are on the supply side: carriers leaving the market, costs rising and enforcement removing non-compliant capacity. Bids built on 2024 pricing will be rejected or quietly ignored by carriers.

## The details

**The rate data.** The Cass Truckload Linehaul Index rose 10.1% year over year in September to its highest reading in four years. It excludes fuel and is weighted toward contract rates. FreightWaves SONAR put contract rates at a 52-week high of $2.72 per mile plus fuel on Oct. 9, up 18% on the year, while spot rates (NTI) were up nearly 50%. The tender reject index sat between 13% and 14%.

**Spend is rising faster than rates.** Cass expenditures rose 20.4% year over year on higher volumes, higher rates and diesel that was 68% above a year earlier. Shipments rose 1.9%, only the second annual increase after 42 straight months of declines. Cass expects October expenditures up about 23%.

**Why capacity is tightening.** SONAR analysis argues that "capacity is leaving faster than freight": tender rejections are up 268% since October 2023 while volumes are up about 9%. Carriers spent roughly three years hauling at rates that barely covered costs. ATRI puts the 2025 average cost per mile at a record $2.336, with non-fuel costs up about 30% from 2021 to 2025. Q3 2026 dry van contract linehaul averaged $2.69, still below late-2021 levels, which is why analysts describe the repricing as only about halfway done.

**Carriers are confident.** At an investor conference, carriers said they hold a position of strength this bid season. Werner guided to a 10% to 13% rate-per-mile increase in its one-way fleet for Q3.

## What procurement teams should do

- **Segment lanes before you bid.** Protect high-volume, high-service lanes with committed carriers. Put low-volume or irregular lanes on shorter terms or a managed spot strategy.
- **Separate fuel from linehaul.** Use a fuel surcharge table tied to the weekly DOE/EIA diesel index so linehaul negotiations aren't distorted by diesel swings.
- **Budget realistically.** If your 2027 freight budget assumes flat rates, revisit it with finance now. Cass and SONAR both point to higher costs into next year.
- **Watch acceptance, not just price.** Track tender acceptance by carrier monthly. A low rate that carriers reject costs more once loads roll to the spot market.
- **Reward consistency.** Carriers favor shippers with predictable volumes, quick dock times and fast payment. Those terms can be worth more than a few cents per mile.

## What to watch next

- **Tender rejections.** Rejections above 13% signal carriers turning down contract freight; watch whether they rise into November.
- **Carrier exits.** Continued capacity loss would keep pressure on 2027 contract rates.
- **Diesel.** Fuel adds to total cost even where linehaul rates stabilize.

## The bigger picture

The freight market is correcting after a long period in which shippers held the leverage. Procurement teams that treat freight as a strategic category, with segmented lanes, indexed fuel terms and carrier scorecards, will secure capacity at a fair price.
