## Why it matters

Many companies buy small, fast shipments from overseas outside the main purchasing channel: spare parts, samples, lab supplies, marketplace orders on a purchasing card. U.S. Customs and Border Protection's proposal would put a bond, an earlier filing deadline and new data requirements on every one of those shipments under $2,500, which adds cost and can add delay if suppliers and couriers are not ready.

## The details

**The proposal.** CBP published the notice of proposed rulemaking on Oct. 8 (FR Doc. 2026-20650, docket USCBP-2026-0298). Comments are due Dec. 7. It covers merchandise valued at $2,500 or less, including international mail, and nothing in it is in effect yet. CBP gave no effective date for a final rule.

**What would change**, according to the [proposed rule](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-20650.pdf) and summaries by GHY International and Customs & International Trade Law:

| Today | Proposed |
|---|---|
| Informal (Type 11) entries can be filed within 15 days after arrival | Filed electronically on or before importation |
| Bonds required for informal entries only in some cases | A single-transaction or continuous bond required for every Type 11 and Type 13 entry |
| Mail entered through a worksheet and pay.gov process | New electronic Type 13 informal mail entry, with sender details and the postal tracking number |
| Goods under certain Chapter 99 provisions above $250 need formal entry | $250 limit removed, so those goods up to $2,500 could use informal entry |
| Formal entry generally required case by case | CBP could require formal entry on a blanket basis by importer, product type or seller |

The proposal also requires the name and address of the final deliver-to party when it differs from the ultimate consignee, and a licensed customs broker as importer of record when the consignee is neither the owner nor the purchaser. Mail under $2,500 that is not entered within 15 days would be treated as abandoned. Minimum liquidated damages for a bond breach would be the greater of the merchandise value or $1,000, according to GHY.

**Why CBP wants it.** CBP says the changes would help it identify high-risk shipments and improve compliance, noting that international mail has historically had higher seizure rates. De minimis was suspended for all countries from Aug. 29, 2025, and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act ends it by statute from July 1, 2027.

**CBP's own impact figures.** About 2.3 million Type 11 entries were filed without a bond between Aug. 1, 2025 and July 31, 2026, or 3.31% of the roughly 70.1 million Type 11 entries CBP projects for 2026. GHY reports CBP found that 68,701 importers filed those unbonded entries, and that for the 59,148 better suited to single-transaction bonds, bond costs would equal 72.66% of their duties, taxes and fees on those entries. For postal entries, CBP assumes a broker fee of about $30 per shipment plus $9.72 in filing time, $39.72 in total. CBP said it cannot certify the rule avoids a significant impact on a substantial number of small businesses.

## What procurement teams should do

- **Find the low-value import spend.** Pull purchasing-card, marketplace and expedited-parts transactions with overseas suppliers, and identify who acts as importer of record on each.
- **Prefer suppliers and couriers that file for you.** For recurring small orders, favor terms where an express carrier or broker files the entry before arrival under its own bond, and confirm that in the order terms.
- **Consolidate where you can.** Fewer, larger shipments under a continuous bond will usually cost less than many single-transaction bonds; ask your customs broker to model it.
- **Tighten supplier data.** Earlier filing means suppliers must send accurate values, descriptions and deliver-to addresses before shipping. Add that to supplier onboarding for overseas vendors.
- **Comment if it affects you.** Companies that depend on international mail or small expedited shipments can file comments by Dec. 7 with their own cost and timing data.

## What to watch next

- **Dec. 7, 2026.** The comment deadline. Watch whether express carriers, brokers and small-business groups push for thresholds or phase-ins.
- **A final rule.** CBP gave no timetable, but the July 1, 2027 statutory end of de minimis is the natural planning date.
- **CBP entry guidance.** Any interim instructions on Type 13 mail entries or blanket formal-entry requirements will show where enforcement focuses first.

## The bigger picture

Low-value imports used to sit outside most procurement controls because they rarely triggered duty or paperwork. The suspension of de minimis, a stack of new tariffs and now this proposal turn every small overseas order into a customs transaction with a cost and a compliance owner. That fits a broader enforcement push: CBP's data analytics and the Justice Department's focus on tariff evasion are raising the risk of getting entries wrong.

For category managers, the practical effect is to move tail spend from overseas marketplaces toward domestic distributors or consolidated programs where the landed-cost math now favors them.
