## Why it matters

LTL pricing usually resets once a year. Carriers are now moving their increases earlier and, in Saia's case, higher. For shippers whose contracts are tied to carrier base tariffs, that means higher costs arrive sooner than budgeted.

## The details

Old Dominion Freight Line announced a 4.9% general rate increase across various tariff codes, effective Oct. 5, a month earlier than last year's increase, which had itself been moved up a month. Vice president of pricing services Greg Lawrence said the increase is designed to offset "continued cost pressures related to real estate, equipment, technology," along with wages and benefits.

Other public LTL carriers have done the same:

| Carrier | Increase | Effective | Change from last year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Old Dominion | 4.9% | Oct. 5, 2026 | One month earlier |
| Saia | 7.1% | July 6, 2026 | 1.2 points higher, three months earlier |
| ArcBest | 5.9% | June 22, 2026 | About six weeks early, an 11-month cadence |

A GRI is an expected average across lanes and weight classes, so individual lanes can rise more or less.

**Demand backdrop.** The ISM manufacturing index was 54.6 in August, its eighth straight month of expansion, and LTL tonnage typically lags the PMI by about three months. FreightWaves described the carriers' pricing outlook as intact, with rising costs as they enter a growth cycle.

## How GRIs reach your invoice

| Contract structure | Effect of a GRI | What to negotiate |
|---|---|---|
| Discount off carrier's current base tariff | Net cost rises with the GRI | Cap annual base increases or freeze the base |
| Fixed rates by lane | No change until renewal | Expect a larger reset at renewal |
| Third-party base tariff (e.g. a neutral rating engine) | Depends on that base | Confirm how and when the base updates |
| Accessorials | Often rise separately | Negotiate key accessorials explicitly |

## What procurement teams should do

- **Check your tariff basis.** Find out whether your LTL contracts float with each carrier's base tariff.
- **Budget the earlier timing.** Increases effective in June, July or October may land inside your 2026 budget year.
- **Negotiate caps.** Seek a cap on annual base increases or a fixed base for the contract term.
- **Review freight class and density.** Accurate classification often saves more than the GRI costs.
- **Consolidate where it pays.** Shift heavier LTL shipments to volume or partial truckload quotes, now that [truckload rates are rising too](/articles/2026-10-14-truckload-contract-rates-52-week-high-bid-season/).

## What to watch next

- **FedEx Freight, XPO and other LTL carriers'** 2026–2027 increases.
- **LTL tonnage**, which should follow manufacturing's recovery with a lag.
- **Accessorial changes** that often accompany GRIs.

## The bigger picture

Across modes, [parcel](/articles/2026-09-21-fedex-2027-rate-increase-5-9-percent-surcharges-parcel/), LTL and truckload carriers are regaining pricing power. Procurement teams should plan 2027 freight budgets for increases across the board and focus negotiations on contract structure, not just discount levels.
