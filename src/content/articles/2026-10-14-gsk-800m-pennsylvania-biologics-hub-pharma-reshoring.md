## Why it matters

Pharmaceutical companies are adding U.S. capacity under pressure from tariffs and tax incentives. For health system and pharmacy procurement, more domestic biologics capacity should eventually mean less exposure to import tariffs and long supply lines, but not before the late 2020s.

## The details

GSK will invest more than $800 million to expand its site in Upper Merion Township, Montgomery County, Pennsylvania, Gov. Josh Shapiro's office announced. Existing buildings will be demolished and replaced with:

- A commercial-scale biologics manufacturing facility for clinical and commercial supply
- A quality control laboratory
- A pilot plant for technology and process development

GSK expects to create more than 300 jobs over five years and retain 4,500 existing jobs. Construction begins in 2027. Regis Simard, GSK's global supply chain president, said the investment will help accelerate the development and supply of future medicines. The state says GSK is eligible for its manufacturing innovation deduction and manufacturing tax credit programs.

The project is GSK's second $800 million Pennsylvania commitment in two years. In October 2024 it pledged the same amount to expand manufacturing and R&D in Lancaster County, creating 200 jobs; that was then its largest U.S. manufacturing investment. Both are part of a $30 billion commitment GSK made in 2025 to expand U.S. supply chain infrastructure and R&D through 2030. GSK previously committed $800 million to a Lancaster County facility in 2024. Eli Lilly and Johnson & Johnson have also invested in Pennsylvania as part of multibillion-dollar U.S. expansion plans. The administration has paired tariffs with targeted tax incentives to encourage this kind of reshoring, and Pennsylvania's own manufacturing tax programs add to the pull.

**The site plan in detail.** According to the governor's office, the Upper Merion project combines commercial production, R&D and digital capabilities on one campus: a commercial-scale biologics plant for clinical and commercial supply, a co-located R&D pilot plant for technology transfer and process development, and a centralized quality control lab with a manufacturing science and technology hub. The state says the Upper Merion and Lancaster County projects together total nearly $1.7 billion.

**Pennsylvania's pitch.** The state counts about 3,900 life sciences companies employing more than 110,000 people. Its 2026–27 budget includes $125 million for life sciences programs covering clinical trial infrastructure, workforce training and collaborative development grants. "GSK could have chosen anywhere in the world to expand production," Gov. Shapiro said.

## Why biologics capacity takes so long

Biologics are made from living cells, so a new plant must be built, validated and approved before it can supply patients. Construction alone typically takes years, followed by process validation and regulatory inspection. Construction in Upper Merion starts in 2027, so supply from the site is unlikely before the end of the decade, in our assessment. Procurement teams should plan around today's supply network for at least the next several contract cycles.

## How to factor reshoring into pharmaceutical sourcing

| Time frame | What changes | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 to 2027 | [Section 232 tariffs](/articles/2026-10-12-section-232-pharma-tariff-now-applies-all-importers/) apply to patented imports; generics exempt for now | Map products by origin and supplier agreement |
| 2027 to 2029 | New U.S. plants under construction; supplier tariff deals change rates | Add rate-change clauses; re-check supplier status quarterly |
| 2029 onward | New capacity begins to qualify; some tariff discounts expire | Revisit dual sourcing and origin requirements in long-term contracts |

## What procurement teams should do

- **Track supplier capacity roadmaps.** Ask pharmaceutical suppliers where future supply for your key products will be made and when new U.S. capacity qualifies.
- **Separate near-term and long-term risk.** Tariff exposure on imported medicines is a 2026–2027 issue; new U.S. plants are a late-decade answer.
- **Use origin in contracts.** Where possible, specify country of origin and tariff pass-through rules in pharmaceutical supply agreements. See our coverage of [Section 232 pharma tariffs](/articles/2026-10-12-section-232-pharma-tariff-now-applies-all-importers/).

## What to watch next

- **Construction start in 2027.** Track whether the project begins on schedule; biologics plants take years to build and qualify.
- **More pharma onshoring.** Further announcements under company tariff agreements will shift where future supply is made.
- **Section 232 pharma rates.** Company-specific onshoring commitments can lower tariff rates for that supplier's products.

## The bigger picture

The onshoring wave in life sciences is real but slow. Procurement teams should treat announcements like this as signals for supplier risk models, not as reasons to relax current contingency plans.
