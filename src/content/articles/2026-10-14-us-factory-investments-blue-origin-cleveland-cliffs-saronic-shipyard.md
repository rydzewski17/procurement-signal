## Why it matters

Plant announcements are early signals of where supplier capacity will loosen or tighten. Several October investments target categories that have been constrained for years, including transformer steel, aerospace components and shipbuilding.

## The details

**Cleveland-Cliffs, Butler Works (Lyndora, Pennsylvania): $200 million.** The expansion, backed by a $75 million Department of Energy grant, adds induction reheat furnaces to raise grain-oriented electrical steel output by up to 25%. The steel goes into transformers. The plant employs about 1,200 people, and Cliffs expects the project to be ready in early 2028.

**Saronic Technologies, Port Alpha (Brownsville, Texas): over $3.2 billion.** A joint venture of JE Dunn and Alberici began construction on a shipyard for manned, autonomy-capable and autonomous vessels, starting on 835 acres with an option to expand to nearly 4,400. The builders call it the first greenfield shipyard in the U.S. since World War II. It is being built in overlapping phases using progressive design-build, with no completion date given.

**Blue Origin, Hutto, Texas: more than $550 million.** A 1.3 million-square-foot campus will serve as the central production hub for satellite and communications programs, solar arrays, avionics and telecom hardware, with more than 2,000 jobs planned. The production will include hardware for its TeraWave and Quartz networks. Texas awarded an $18.2 million grant for the project.

**Smaller aerospace and industrial investments:**

- **PPG, Huntsville, Alabama, $70 million:** aircraft cockpit and passenger windows in a new 112,000-square-foot facility
- **Accu-Precision, Colorado Springs:** more than $68 million for aerospace parts, sheet metal and assemblies, with up to 133 jobs
- **Re:Build, Rock Hill, South Carolina, $20 million:** tripling its local footprint for aerospace, defense, energy and robotics components, with 150 jobs by 2028
- **Xingyu Automotive Lighting, Landis, North Carolina, $50 million:** its first U.S. plant, making injection-molded lighting components, with 300 to 500 jobs over several years

**More detail on the incentives.** Blue Origin's $18.2 million Texas grant came with a $10,000 incentive tied to hiring veterans, and the Hutto campus sits in Constellation Park, according to Manufacturing Dive. Cleveland-Cliffs describes Butler Works as the only producer of grain-oriented electrical steel in the U.S.; its new induction reheat furnaces are also meant to improve quality, reduce slab losses and cut hot-rolling emissions. Accu-Precision's 110,000-square-foot facility, driven by new contracts from an undisclosed aerospace customer, qualifies for a $1.7 million job creation tax credit over eight years. Re:Build's 130,000-square-foot Rock Hill site will add a clean room for composite fabrication, a heat-treat oven, a quality lab and machining, with a $100,000 state grant. Xingyu's Landis plant will make injection-molded headlight and taillight components.

## Why transformer steel matters beyond utilities

Grain-oriented electrical steel is the core material in power and distribution transformers. Lead times for transformers have been long as utilities, data center builders and manufacturers expand power capacity. More domestic steel won't shorten lead times until 2028, but it gives transformer makers a second domestic option and may reduce exposure to [Section 232 tariffs on imported steel](/cost-monitor/steel/). Any company building new facilities, data centers or electrified production should track this.

## How to turn new capacity into qualified supply

New plants rarely help the first year they open. For regulated or engineered parts, a new supplier usually has to pass several steps before it can ship production parts:

1. **Early conversation.** Contact the company's business development team while the plant is being built and share expected volumes.
2. **Capability and quality review.** Confirm certifications the part needs, such as AS9100 for aerospace or IATF 16949 for automotive.
3. **Site audit.** Visit once equipment is installed, not before.
4. **First-article or sample approval.** Test parts from production tooling.
5. **Ramp with a safety net.** Keep your current source for a set period until the new one proves delivery.

This is general sourcing practice. Engineering and quality teams should set the requirements for each part.

## Watch for demand, not just supply

Some of these projects add demand before they add supply. A shipyard and a satellite campus will buy steel, electronics, machining and composites for years before their own output reaches the market. Procurement teams buying in the same supply base, especially in Texas, should expect more competition for sub-tier suppliers and skilled labor.

## What procurement teams should do

- **Electrical equipment categories:** Add 2028 domestic electrical steel capacity to your supply outlook and ask transformer suppliers how it affects their lead times.
- **Aerospace and defense categories:** New sub-tier capacity in Texas, Alabama, Colorado and South Carolina may open qualification opportunities. Start supplier development conversations early.
- **Labor-intensive categories:** Thousands of new manufacturing jobs in Texas and the Southeast can tighten local labor markets and supplier wage costs.
- **Track timelines, not announcements.** Build capacity into plans only when suppliers commit to qualification dates.

## What to watch next

- **Completion dates.** Cleveland-Cliffs targets early 2028; the shipyard has no completion date.
- **Supplier qualification.** New aerospace and defense sub-tier capacity will need customer qualification before it helps lead times.
- **Regional labor markets.** Watch wage pressure in Texas and the Southeast as thousands of manufacturing jobs are added.

## The bigger picture

U.S. manufacturing investment is concentrating in defense, aerospace, energy infrastructure and reshored components. The same categories are seeing heavy demand from [defense production ramps](/articles/2026-10-14-raytheon-30-7b-multiyear-missile-contracts-defense-supplier-ramp/). Procurement teams should track new capacity as closely as new demand.
