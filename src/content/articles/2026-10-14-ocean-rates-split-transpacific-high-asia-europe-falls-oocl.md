## Why it matters

Ocean freight is moving in two directions at once. European shippers are regaining leverage as rates fall, while U.S. importers still face rates several times last year's. Contract strategy for 2027 should differ by trade lane, not follow one global view.

## The details

**Rates (Xeneta, as of Oct. 7).** Far East–U.S. West Coast spot rates slipped $2 per FEU on Oct. 7, the first daily decline in weeks, but remained at $8,336 per FEU, 1.1% above Sept. 30. Asia–U.S. East Coast averaged $11,512 per FEU, up 0.5% on the week. On Asia–Europe, Far East–North Europe averaged $3,645 per FEU, down 34% since July 1, and Asia–Mediterranean $4,007, down 43%. North Europe–U.S. East Coast fell 1.1% to $2,854.

Xeneta's Peter Sand said the decline has slowed but the trend is still down, and forwarders are getting better deals in long-term contract talks. European shippers are negotiating contracts as short-term rates fall.

**Carrier results.** OOCL, majority-owned by Cosco Shipping Holdings, reported third-quarter liner revenue of $3.28 billion, up 45.1% year over year, on volume up 9% to 2.12 million TEU. Average revenue per TEU rose 33.1%. Trans-Pacific revenue jumped 71.8% to $1.44 billion on 13.1% more volume. Asia–Europe revenue rose 45% and Trans-Atlantic 11.9%. In every region, revenue grew faster than volume:

| OOCL trade, Q3 2026 | Revenue | Revenue change | Volume (TEU) | Volume change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trans-Pacific | $1.44 billion | +71.8% | 595,139 | +13.1% |
| Asia–Europe | $695.8 million | +45.0% | 399,162 | +15.4% |
| Intra-Asia and Australasia | $947.6 million | +23.7% | 981,336 | +4.6% |
| Trans-Atlantic | $201.8 million | +11.9% | 147,008 | +7.3% |

Over the first nine months of 2026, OOCL's liner revenue rose 18.9% to $7.96 billion on 6.5% more volume, and trans-Pacific revenue was up 27%. The update did not include costs or earnings, so profitability can't be judged from it.

**What the numbers imply.** Dividing revenue by volume, OOCL earned roughly $2,420 per TEU on the trans-Pacific in the third quarter, against about $1,590 a year earlier, a rise of about 52%. On Asia–Europe it earned roughly $1,740 per TEU. These are our calculations from OOCL's figures, and they include all contract and spot business, not just spot rates.

## What procurement teams should do

- **Segment ocean strategy by lane.** On Asia–Europe, push for lower long-term rates now. On the trans-Pacific, avoid locking today's highs into a 12-month fixed rate.
- **Use index-linked contracts.** Tie part of your volume to a published rate index with floors and ceilings, so you benefit as markets ease.
- **Benchmark before you sign.** Compare carrier offers against current spot and long-term indexes for each lane.
- **Pair price with reliability.** With [on-time arrivals at 27%](/articles/2026-10-14-ocean-schedule-reliability-27-percent-vessel-delays-six-days/), trade volume commitments for service terms, not price alone.

## Questions to put to carriers and forwarders for 2027

1. What share of our volume would you price as fixed, and what share index-linked, and to which index?
2. What floor and ceiling would apply to index-linked rates?
3. Which services and sailings will you protect from blank sailings, and what happens to our allocation if a sailing is cancelled?
4. How will bunker surcharges be calculated and adjusted, separately from the base rate?
5. What on-time performance will you commit to on our lanes, and what credit applies if you miss it?

On Asia–Europe, where rates have fallen sharply, it is reasonable to push for a lower fixed rate now. On the trans-Pacific, where carriers' revenue per container is up by roughly half, avoid fixing today's level for a full year without a review point.

## What to watch next

- **Trans-Pacific peak.** Watch whether the first daily decline on Oct. 7 becomes a trend as peak season ends.
- **Blank sailings.** Carriers are expected to blank at least 13% of Far East–North America capacity in October to support rates.
- **2027 contract season.** Asia–Europe deals are being signed now; trans-Pacific contracts are typically negotiated in early spring.

## The bigger picture

Carrier revenue growing far faster than volume means pricing, not demand, is doing the work on U.S. trades. That margin is the cushion procurement teams can negotiate against as more capacity returns. The European slide shows how quickly leverage can shift once demand growth slows.
