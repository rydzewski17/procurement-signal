## Why it matters

A combined Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern would create the first single-line railroad from coast to coast. For companies that ship by rail, the merger could change routings, gateways, service and pricing power. The review schedule is now set, and the window for shippers to ask for protective conditions closes Nov. 18.

## The details

The Surface Transportation Board adopted a procedural schedule on Aug. 18, taking the revised merger application out of abeyance and starting the merits review, including environmental review. The Board said the decision "does not constitute a ruling on the merits." It denied the railroads' request to speed up a separate proceeding on divesting control of the Terminal Railroad Association of St. Louis.

**How we got here:**

| Date | Step |
|---|---|
| April 30, 2026 | Revised application filed |
| May 28, 2026 | Accepted but held in abeyance pending more information |
| July 7 and July 27 | Supplemental information filed |
| Aug. 18 | Procedural schedule adopted; merits review begins |

**The schedule ahead:**

| Date | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Sept. 30, 2026 | Notices of intent to participate (extended from Sept. 4) |
| Oct. 5, 2026 | Petitions for waiver or clarification |
| Nov. 13, 2026 | Responsive environmental information and verified statements |
| Nov. 18, 2026 | Comments, protests, requests for conditions, opposition and responsive applications |
| Dec. 3, 2026 | Preliminary Justice Department and Transportation Department comments |
| Feb. 16, 2027 | Responses and rebuttals |
| March 29, 2027 | Rebuttals supporting responsive applications |
| May 28, 2027 | Final briefs; a public hearing is expected before this |
| Within 90 days of record closing | Final decision |

The Board extended the notice-of-intent deadline at the National League of Cities' request, calling it "reasonable" given the transaction's scope.

**What the Board will weigh.** Major rail mergers need STB approval, and the Board will consider the transaction's effect on competition, service and the public interest, including any conditions that could address harms, through the comment and hearing process laid out in the schedule. Environmental review runs in parallel. The Justice Department and the Transportation Department can file their own views, with preliminary comments due Dec. 3.

**A related decision.** The Board declined the railroads' request to speed up a separate proceeding on divesting control of the Terminal Railroad Association of St. Louis, which handles interchange traffic at one of the country's key rail gateways. Shippers that route through St. Louis should watch that proceeding alongside the merger itself.

**Why the schedule matters for contracts.** A final decision isn't expected until well into 2027, and integration would follow. Rail contracts signed for 2027 and 2028 should anticipate the possibility of a combined railroad, with terms covering service levels, routing and assignment if the deal is approved.

**Smaller dates in the schedule.** The Board also required the railroads to refile unfiltered workpapers by Aug. 28, set Sept. 9 for descriptions of any anticipated responsive applications and Dec. 18 for a Federal Register notice of accepted responsive applications, according to Railway Age. The Board said the supplemental filings "ensured the Board had the information necessary to thoroughly evaluate the proposed transaction." The hearing date will be set in a later decision. Responsive applications are how other railroads or parties ask for rights such as trackage or line sales as a condition of approval, so the Dec. 18 notice will show whether any competitor is seeking access to UP or NS lines.

## How to map your rail exposure

Before deciding whether to file, procurement and logistics teams need a lane-by-lane view. A simple table is enough to start:

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Origin and destination | Shows whether the move touches UP, NS or both |
| Railroads serving each end | Identifies captive locations served by only one railroad |
| Interchange point | Lanes that interchange between UP and NS today would become single-line |
| Annual carloads and spend | Sizes the exposure for internal decisions and any filing |
| Contract expiry | Shows which rates renew during the review or integration |
| Truck or intermodal alternative | Tells you how much leverage you have if service slips |

**Who gains and who could lose.** Lanes that interchange between UP and NS today could benefit from single-line service, with fewer handoffs and potentially faster transit. Shippers whose competitive options depend on another railroad interchanging with UP or NS have the most to watch, because a combined railroad may prefer to keep traffic on its own network. This is our general read of how end-to-end rail mergers affect shippers, not a judgment on this deal.

## Contract terms to review now

- **Assignment and change of control.** Whether your contract transfers to the combined company unchanged.
- **Service metrics.** Transit time, car order fulfillment and dwell, with remedies if they slip during integration.
- **Rate protection.** How long current rates hold and how increases are calculated.
- **Routing.** Whether the railroad can change routings or gateways without your agreement.
- **Exit rights.** Whether you can terminate if service falls below agreed levels.

## What to put in a request for conditions

Shippers that depend on either railroad can ask the Board to attach conditions. Common asks include:

- Keeping existing interchange gateways open with competitive rates
- Service performance commitments and reporting
- Protection for captive shippers served by only one railroad
- Limits on rate increases on affected lanes for a set period

These are typical categories of conditions in rail mergers, not a forecast of what the Board will grant. Work with counsel or your trade association.

## What procurement teams should do

- **Map your rail exposure.** List lanes, origins and destinations served by UP, NS or both, and any interchange points.
- **Decide whether to participate.** If service or competition on your lanes could change, coordinate with your industry association before Nov. 18.
- **Review rail contracts.** Check assignment, change-of-control and service terms.
- **Keep truck and intermodal alternatives qualified.** Integration periods can disrupt service even when mergers are approved.

## What to watch next

- **The Nov. 18 filings**, which will show which shippers and railroads oppose the deal or seek conditions.
- **Justice and Transportation Department comments** in December.
- **The public hearing** in 2027.

## The bigger picture

Transport suppliers are consolidating across modes, from [freight brokers](/articles/2026-10-14-ch-robinson-rxo-merger-what-shippers-should-do/) to railroads. Procurement teams should treat these deals as supplier-risk events: map exposure early and use the regulatory process when it's available.
