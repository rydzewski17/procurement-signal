## Why it matters

Freight brokers are suppliers, and supplier consolidation changes your leverage. If you use both C.H. Robinson and RXO, your broker diversification may become one relationship. Integration will run through the 2027 bid season, when [truckload contract rates are already rising](/articles/2026-10-14-truckload-contract-rates-52-week-high-bid-season/).

## The details

**The terms.** C.H. Robinson announced on Oct. 5 that it will acquire RXO in a cash-and-stock deal with an implied value of about $5.8 billion. RXO shareholders receive $17.25 in cash plus 0.0856 C.H. Robinson shares for each RXO share, or can elect all cash ($30.25) or all stock, subject to proration so the total is about 57% cash and 43% stock. The $30.25 implied price is a 29% premium to RXO's Oct. 2 close. RXO shareholders will own about 11% of the combined company, which will have an enterprise value above $25 billion.

**Timing and approvals.** The companies expect to close in the first half of 2027, subject to regulatory approval and an RXO shareholder vote. MFN Partners, which holds about 17% of RXO, has agreed to vote in favor. Either side faces a $185 million breakup fee if the deal collapses. The cash portion will be funded with new debt, and C.H. Robinson will pause share buybacks until leverage falls to its target range.

**The savings case.** C.H. Robinson is targeting about $300 million in net annual cost synergies within two years of close, from cost-to-serve, operating efficiencies, shared services and third-party spend. RXO will be integrated mainly into C.H. Robinson's North American Surface Transportation business. For scale, C.H. Robinson alone serves about 75,000 customers with 450,000 contract carriers and 37 million shipments a year.

**The skeptics.** The price is roughly 42 times EBITDA, against 8 to 13 times for comparable companies, according to a FreightWaves interview with Matthew Leffler, an attorney who covers the freight industry. Leffler said antitrust risk looks minimal but questioned the savings target because RXO is already lean and still integrating Coyote, which it bought from UPS. He said almost every merger of this size misses targets like this.

**Market share.** The combined company would handle about 20% of the brokered freight market, though C.H. Robinson says its share of the overall transportation market is in single digits.

**Liabilities and the broker market.** All of RXO's legal liabilities transfer at close, including litigation tied to serious motor carrier accidents. Leffler described a market of rising insurance costs, a shrinking carrier pool and growing litigation exposure that is pushing mid-size brokers toward the exit. Panelists said brokers ranked roughly 20 to 50 by size could be the next to consolidate. Both companies also run pools of drop trailers, sometimes 3,000 to 4,000 units each, a service many shippers rely on.

By our calculation, the exchange terms imply a C.H. Robinson share price of about $152 at announcement, since $13 of the $30.25 price comes from 0.0856 shares.

**Where the $300 million could come from.** The companies named cost-to-serve, operating efficiencies, shared services and third-party spend. For shippers, the parts to watch are cost-to-serve and operations, because those are where account teams, carrier sales and technology are combined. Savings there can mean fewer people on your account or a move to the acquirer's systems.

## What this means for shippers

| If you... | Exposure | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Use both brokers on the same lanes | Lost diversification and leverage | High: requalify alternatives before your next bid |
| Use one of them heavily | Account team and system changes | Medium: lock in service terms in writing |
| Rely on drop-trailer programs | Trailer pool consolidation | Medium: confirm trailer counts by site |
| Use mid-size brokers | Possible future acquisition | Watch: check financial health and ownership |

## What procurement teams should do

- **Measure your exposure.** Add up spend and loads with both brokers. If the combined share crosses your concentration threshold, plan to diversify.
- **Lock in service terms.** Before close, confirm account teams, rate commitments, tender acceptance targets and escalation contacts in writing.
- **Review assignment clauses.** Check whether your broker agreements allow assignment on a merger and what notice you get if service teams or systems change.
- **Watch performance during integration.** Track on-time pickup, tender acceptance and invoice accuracy monthly through the first year after close.
- **Refresh your broker bench.** Qualify one or two alternative brokers or asset carriers on key lanes, especially if mid-size brokers you use are exit candidates.
- **Check trailer pools.** Drop-trailer programs are a differentiator; confirm that capacity carries over.

## Questions for your C.H. Robinson or RXO account team

1. Will our account team, escalation contacts and service model change after close?
2. Which technology platform will we tender and track loads on, and when would a change happen?
3. Will our current rates and tender acceptance commitments carry over for the contract term?
4. How will drop-trailer pools at our sites be handled?
5. What share of our freight moves on the same carriers today, and will that carrier base change?

## What to watch next

- **RXO shareholder vote and regulatory review.** With MFN Partners committed, the vote looks likely to pass, but watch for opposition over the price.
- **Integration announcements.** Watch for changes to account teams, technology platforms and carrier networks after close in 2027.
- **Mid-size broker exits.** Brokers ranked roughly 20 to 50 by size could be next; check the financial health of the mid-size brokers you use.

## The bigger picture

Brokerage is consolidating as costs and legal risk rise. Fewer, larger brokers can mean better technology and scale, but also less choice and, as Leffler warned, a market of one large public broker surrounded by private equity-owned competitors with less transparency. Procurement teams should treat the broker base like any concentrated supplier category: diversified, scorecarded and reviewed before the market changes, not after.
