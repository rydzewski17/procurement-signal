## Why it matters

Freight brokers are suppliers, and supplier consolidation changes your leverage. If you use both C.H. Robinson and RXO, your broker diversification may become one relationship. Integration can also disrupt service during peak season.

## The details

C.H. Robinson's proposed acquisition of RXO is valued at about $5.8 billion, roughly 42 times EBITDA, as a stock deal. The combined company would hold about 20% of the brokered freight market, though C.H. Robinson says its share of the overall transportation market is in single digits. Either party faces a $185 million breakup fee if the deal collapses. The deal requires regulatory clearance and an RXO shareholder vote.

C.H. Robinson has pledged $300 million in cost savings within two years. Analysts on a FreightWaves panel questioned that target because RXO is already lean and still integrating its Coyote acquisition. Dan Bailey of Nexcade said C.H. Robinson's lean model has delivered results but warned that limited customer overlap and different operating practices create execution risk.

Because it's a stock deal, RXO's existing liabilities, including litigation tied to motor carrier accidents, would transfer at close. Panelists said rising insurance costs, a shrinking carrier pool and litigation exposure are pushing mid-size brokers toward the exit, and that brokers ranked roughly 20 to 50 by size could be the next consolidation targets.

## What procurement teams should do

- **Measure your exposure.** Add up spend and loads with both brokers. If the combined share crosses your concentration threshold, plan to diversify.
- **Lock in service terms.** Before close, confirm account teams, rate commitments, tender acceptance targets and escalation contacts in writing.
- **Watch performance during integration.** Track on-time pickup, tender acceptance and invoice accuracy monthly through the first year after close.
- **Refresh your broker bench.** Qualify one or two alternative brokers or asset carriers on key lanes, especially if mid-size brokers you use are exit candidates.
- **Check trailer pools.** Drop-trailer programs are a differentiator; confirm that capacity carries over.

## What to watch next

- **RXO shareholder vote.** Analysts see shareholder dissent as a more plausible obstacle than antitrust review.
- **Integration announcements.** Watch for changes to account teams, technology platforms and carrier networks in the first months after close.
- **Mid-size broker exits.** Brokers ranked roughly 20 to 50 by size could be next; check the financial health of the mid-size brokers you use.

## The bigger picture

Brokerage is consolidating as costs and legal risk rise. Fewer, larger brokers can mean better technology and scale, but also less choice. Procurement teams should treat the broker base like any concentrated supplier category: diversified, scorecarded and reviewed before the market changes, not after.
