## Why it matters

Fuel is the fastest-moving part of freight cost, and diesel is up about 68% year over year according to Cass. Two policy moves in October were billed as relief. Neither is likely to change what procurement teams pay in fuel surcharges this quarter, so budgets and carrier contracts should plan for elevated diesel.

## The details

**Prices.** The DOE/EIA average retail diesel price was $6.179 a gallon effective Oct. 12, down 2 cents, the third straight weekly decline. AAA's daily average fell for 17 days from Sept. 22 but rose 3.3 cents in the two days to Oct. 14, to $6.2997. Diesel set an all-time record above $6.50 in September before easing.

**The Russia deal.** The administration announced that Russia will supply more than 300,000 metric tons of diesel to the world market immediately, about 2.2 million barrels, with 500,000 tonnes in November and 1 million tonnes after that. U.S. non-jet distillate demand runs about 3.65 million barrels a day. Masha Kotkin of the Center for Strategic and International Studies told S&P Global the volumes are a small share of global demand and likely to make little difference to U.S. prices. ULSD futures rose after the announcement.

**The red-dye order.** An executive order published Oct. 9 directs Treasury to consider deferring federal excise tax obligations and waiving penalties for red-dye diesel used on roads, with a proposed deferral from Oct. 5 to Dec. 31. Many states prohibit dyed diesel on their roads, and wholesale and retail networks rarely keep it separate. Breakthrough Fuel rated the retail price effect as low.

**Other factors.** Strategic stock releases by G7 and IEA members, drone strikes on Russian refineries and uncertainty about flows through the Strait of Hormuz continue to drive volatility.

## What procurement teams should do

- **Index fuel surcharges.** Tie carrier fuel surcharges to the weekly DOE/EIA diesel price with a published table, and audit invoices against it.
- **Budget for elevated fuel.** Plan 2027 freight budgets with a fuel scenario near current levels and a volatility buffer, not a return to 2025 prices.
- **Reduce fuel exposure.** Consolidate shipments, raise load factors and evaluate intermodal on long lanes, where road-to-rail conversion has accelerated.
- **Don't count on red-dye savings.** Interstate carriers face state-by-state rules and compliance costs that likely outweigh the tax deferral.

## What to watch next

- **Weekly EIA prices.** Three straight weekly declines could reverse; AAA's daily average already ticked up in mid-October.
- **Russian volumes.** Watch whether the promised 500,000 tonnes in November actually reach the market and move futures.
- **Winter demand.** Heating oil season adds pressure on distillates from November, which typically supports diesel prices.

## The bigger picture

Diesel is being driven by global supply disruptions and refining constraints, not by policy announcements. Procurement teams should assume volatility and manage it through contract structure.
