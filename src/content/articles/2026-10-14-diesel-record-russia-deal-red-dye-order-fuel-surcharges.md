## Why it matters

Fuel is the fastest-moving part of freight cost, and diesel was 68% above a year earlier in September, according to Cass. Two policy moves in October were billed as relief. Neither is likely to change what procurement teams pay in fuel surcharges this quarter, so budgets and carrier contracts should plan for elevated diesel.

## The details

**Prices.** The DOE/EIA average retail diesel price was $6.179 a gallon effective Oct. 12, down 2 cents, the third straight weekly decline. AAA's daily average fell for 17 days from Sept. 22 but rose 3.3 cents in the two days to Oct. 14, to $6.2997. Diesel set an all-time record above $6.50 in September before easing only modestly.

**The Russia deal.** On Oct. 9, President Trump said Russia would supply more than 300,000 metric tons of diesel immediately, about 2.2 million barrels, with 500,000 tonnes in November and 1 million tonnes after that. Treasury issued a sanctions waiver allowing the sale and import of Russian diesel through April 7, 2027. For scale, U.S. non-jet distillate demand runs about 3.65 million barrels a day.

Analysts doubt the volumes will show up or matter. S&P Global Energy CERA estimates Russia has exported only 100,000 to 150,000 barrels a day of diesel in recent months, with roughly half its refining capacity offline after Ukrainian attacks. Rachel Ziemba of Horizon Engage said the October and November volumes look like recent exports, and that scaling up would require refinery repairs and an energy ceasefire. "The amount of diesel Russia can provide would be a drop in the bucket compared to global demand," Masha Kotkin of the Center for Strategic and International Studies told S&P Global. ULSD futures rose after the announcement.

**Legal hurdles.** A bipartisan group of six senators led by Sen. Jeanne Shaheen said Congress banned Russian energy imports in 2022 and that the administration did not consult Congress as the law requires. Ellen Wald of Transversal Consulting said the ban can be lifted only after the president shows Congress evidence of a Russia–Ukraine agreement to withdraw forces, followed by a 90-day wait, so Russian diesel could not legally enter the U.S. in October or November without new legislation.

**The red-dye order.** An executive order published Oct. 9 directs Treasury to consider deferring federal excise tax obligations and waiving penalties for red-dye diesel used on roads, with a proposed deferral from Oct. 5 to Dec. 31. Many states prohibit dyed diesel on their roads, and wholesale and retail networks rarely keep it separate. Breakthrough Fuel rated the retail price effect as low.

**Other factors.** Strategic stock releases by G7 and IEA members, continuing drone strikes on Russian refineries, talk of a possible U.S. export ban and uncertainty about flows through the Strait of Hormuz all add to volatility.

## What it means for your surcharges

Most truckload fuel surcharges follow a simple formula: the weekly DOE/EIA price minus a base price, divided by an assumed fuel economy. The table below uses a $1.25 base and 6.5 miles per gallon, common contract assumptions. Your contracts may differ.

| Weekly diesel price | Surcharge per mile | Fuel cost on a 1,000-mile load |
|---|---|---|
| $5.50 | $0.65 | $654 |
| $6.18 (Oct. 12) | $0.76 | $758 |
| $6.50 (September record) | $0.81 | $808 |

By our calculation, every 10-cent move in diesel changes the surcharge by about 1.5 cents a mile under these assumptions. That is why the surcharge table, not the headline price, decides what you pay.

## What procurement teams should do

- **Index fuel surcharges.** Tie carrier fuel surcharges to the weekly DOE/EIA diesel price with a published table, and audit invoices against it.
- **Standardize base and fuel economy.** Different base prices and mpg assumptions across carriers make bids hard to compare. Normalize them in your RFP.
- **Budget for elevated fuel.** Plan 2027 freight budgets with a fuel scenario near current levels and a volatility buffer, not a return to 2025 prices.
- **Reduce fuel exposure.** Consolidate shipments, raise load factors and evaluate intermodal on long lanes, where road-to-rail conversion has accelerated as fuel prices surged.
- **Don't count on red-dye savings.** Interstate carriers face state-by-state rules and compliance costs that likely outweigh the tax deferral.

## What to watch next

- **Weekly EIA prices.** Three straight weekly declines could reverse; AAA's daily average already ticked up in mid-October.
- **Russian volumes and the legal fight.** Watch whether Congress challenges the waiver and whether November volumes actually reach the market.
- **Winter demand.** Heating oil season adds pressure on distillates from November, which typically supports diesel prices.

## The bigger picture

Diesel is being driven by global supply disruptions and refining constraints, not by policy announcements. Analysts say only a lasting energy ceasefire that lets Russia repair refineries would change the picture, and that would take many months. Procurement teams should assume volatility and manage it through contract structure.
