## Why it matters

Freight procurement still runs on email. When a forwarder or broker can price your shipment in minutes instead of hours, you get more competitive quotes and fewer missed bookings. When the same tools reconcile invoices against contracts, you catch detention and accessorial charges that slip through today.

## The details

In a FreightWaves interview, Dan Bailey, co-founder and CEO of logistics AI company Nexcade, said about 90% of freight quote requests still arrive by email. His company's agents read inboxes, classify the freight, pull shipment details and run lookups against contract rates, spot markets and overseas agent portals. The goal is for an operator to start the day with 10 to 15 quotes ready for a pricing decision instead of working through each request by hand.

Bailey said teams using the platform have doubled the number of files each person handles. About 75% of current volume is air and ocean, and 25% is road freight in the U.S. and Europe.

His view on where returns come from:

- **Context is the hardest part.** Most of the value sits in tacit knowledge about lanes, customers and agents. Turning that into context the AI can use is the main obstacle.
- **Risk reduction compounds.** Reconciliation and exception handling cut demurrage, detention and unexpected charges, with the effect becoming significant over 12 to 18 months.
- **Speed shows up in revenue.** Win rates by response time and margin by lane quantify the commercial effect.
- **Shadow AI is already happening.** Without approved tools, staff will use general chatbots anyway.

## What procurement teams should do

- **Ask your forwarders and brokers how they quote.** Faster, AI-assisted quoting should mean more competitive responses. Include response time in your provider scorecards.
- **Target accessorials first.** If you are buying AI for freight audit, start where leakage is measurable: detention, demurrage and charges that don't match contract terms.
- **Document lane context.** Write down the routing rules, customer requirements and exceptions your team knows by heart. Any AI tool, yours or your provider's, needs it.
- **Run small pilots before big rollouts.** Bailey recommends pairing a leadership-led data foundation with small team experiments that give several chances to learn before scaling.

## What to watch next

- **Measured results.** Ask vendors for before-and-after data on quote turnaround, files per person and exception costs over 12 to 18 months.
- **Data rights.** As AI providers build on your shipment data, watch contract terms on ownership and model training.
- **Shadow AI policies.** Expect more companies to approve specific tools so staff stop pasting rates into general chatbots.

## The bigger picture

Every major forwarder and broker is announcing AI capabilities, and the volume of announcements is hard to evaluate. For shippers, the useful test is simple: are quotes faster, are invoices more accurate, and are fewer charges disputed?
