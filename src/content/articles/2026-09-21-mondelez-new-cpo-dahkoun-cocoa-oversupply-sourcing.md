## Why it matters

Food and consumer goods companies are putting commodity specialists in the top procurement seat. Mondelēz's choice of its cocoa chief, as the market for its most volatile input turns, shows how closely procurement leadership is tied to commodity strategy. For suppliers and peers, it also signals how the company may approach cocoa contracting as prices ease.

## The details

Mondelēz, maker of Oreo and Chips Ahoy, promoted Zakaria Dahkoun to chief procurement officer effective Aug. 1. He most recently served as senior vice president of the cocoa enterprise and earlier as SVP of global direct materials sourcing. He has more than 13 years at Mondelēz and more than 21 years in procurement. "His experience covers end-to-end procurement across diverse categories and geographies," spokesperson Desiree Battaglia said.

He succeeds Thomas Gaengler, CPO for four years, who left to become chief operating officer at chocolate maker Barry Callebaut, a major cocoa supplier.

**The cocoa backdrop.** Years of volatile cocoa prices pushed Mondelēz to shift some of its product mix toward items less reliant on chocolate. Now, after producers expanded output, the market is oversupplied, according to Supply Chain Dive. "I think cocoa in terms of supply and demand for the years to come is in a much different situation and a much better one," chief operating officer Luca Zaramella said at a Barclays investor conference in September, adding that recent production "is more than sufficient to cover the deficit." He also said Mondelēz plans a major Oreo overhaul in 2027 with new formulas.

**Why the predecessor's move matters.** Thomas Gaengler's move from Mondelēz CPO to chief operating officer at Barry Callebaut, one of the world's largest cocoa and chocolate suppliers, is a reminder that procurement leaders often cross to the supplier side. For companies that buy from Barry Callebaut, it means the supplier's operations are now led by someone who knows a major customer's procurement strategy from the inside.

**What the cocoa shift means for the role.** Dahkoun takes over as the company's most volatile input moves from shortage to surplus. Mondelēz shifted some of its product mix away from chocolate-heavy products when prices spiked; with supply recovering, the procurement question becomes how quickly to rebalance, and how to contract for cocoa so the company benefits from lower prices without locking in exposure if the market tightens again.

## What it means for procurement teams

- **Commodity cycles shape leadership.** When an input dominates cost, companies promote the people who manage it.
- **Easing markets shift negotiation.** If you buy cocoa or cocoa-based ingredients, falling prices are the time to revisit fixed-price terms set at the peak and to build index links into new contracts.
- **Supplier relationships move with people.** A former CPO now running operations at a key supplier can change how that relationship works. Keep relationship maps current.

## What to watch next

- **Cocoa contract renewals** for 2027 as suppliers and manufacturers reset prices.
- **Mondelēz's 2027 product reformulation**, which may change ingredient demand.
- **More CPO appointments** from commodity roles across food and consumer goods.

## The bigger picture

The cocoa cycle shows how quickly a shortage can become a surplus once prices pull in new supply. Procurement teams that locked in long fixed prices at the peak now carry above-market costs; those that used index-linked or shorter terms can capture the decline.
