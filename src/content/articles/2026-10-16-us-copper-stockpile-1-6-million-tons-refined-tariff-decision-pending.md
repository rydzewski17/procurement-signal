## Why it matters

A year of tariff-driven buying has left the U.S. holding a large share of the world's visible copper, while the refined-copper tariff that drove it is still undecided. For wire, cable, motors, HVAC and electrical components, that means pricing should reflect ample domestic metal and a shrinking U.S. premium, not a tariff that hasn't been imposed.

## The details

**The stockpile.** Macquarie estimates total U.S. copper stocks, on and off exchange, at about 1.6 million tons, [ScrapMonster reported](https://www.scrapmonster.com/news/copper/u.s.-copper-stockpile-hits-1.6m-tons-2026-10-15/100255) on Oct. 15. COMEX warehouses hold more than 714,000 tons, up nearly 750% since the end of February 2025, and LME warehouses in New Orleans and Baltimore hold about 130,000 tons, up about 44% since mid-August. COMEX now holds about 70% of the copper in COMEX, LME and Shanghai exchange warehouses combined, which total just over 1 million tons.

For scale: U.S. mines produced about 1.0 million tons of recoverable copper in 2025, according to the U.S. Geological Survey, and the U.S. imported more than 1.62 million tons, up 80% from 2024, Reuters reported, as cited by ScrapMonster.

**The tariff that hasn't happened.** Since 2025, semi-finished copper products such as pipe, wire and rod have carried a 50% Section 232 duty, while refined cathode is exempt, according to Crux Investor. Commerce previously recommended a universal tariff on refined copper of 15% from Jan. 1, 2027 and 30% from Jan. 1, 2028. Commerce's updated market assessment was due June 30, 2026, and Bloodstone Research says that window passed without an announcement. ScrapMonster puts it plainly: Jan. 1 is a proposed start date, not an automatic deadline.

**The premium is fading.** The COMEX premium over LME copper peaked above $3,000 a ton in July 2025 and was about $310 on Oct. 15, a drop of almost 90%, according to ScrapMonster. Bloodstone found some nearby COMEX contracts trading roughly level with or below LME at the end of September, and said the incentive to keep shipping cathode into U.S. warehouses has narrowed substantially. Macquarie's Alice Fox told ScrapMonster the arbitrage could weaken further if January passes without a decision.

**Prices are still high.** LME copper traded between about $14,296 and $14,482 a ton on Oct. 16, Newsquawk reported, after Bloodstone recorded $14,415 on Oct. 8. Macquarie forecasts a 2026 global refined-copper surplus of 223,000 tons, with larger surpluses possible from 2027. Bloodstone notes that the 2026 benchmark treatment charge for smelters settled at $0 a ton, down from $21.25 in 2025, which in our reading points to tight mine supply.

**Scrap.** The 2025 copper proclamation also directs that 25% of high-quality U.S.-produced copper scrap be sold domestically, ScrapMonster reported. Scrap was excluded from the original import tariffs.

## Three tariff scenarios for copper-intensive categories

| Scenario | What happens to the U.S. premium | What it means for suppliers' pricing |
|---|---|---|
| 15% refined tariff confirmed for Jan. 1 | Likely widens again as imports reprice | Suppliers will seek a tariff surcharge; insist it applies only to imported cathode content |
| Decision delayed again | Likely keeps narrowing as stocks are drawn | Push for COMEX-based pricing to converge with LME and for premiums to come out |
| Tariff dropped | Premium could disappear | U.S. stockpile overhang favors procurement teams in 2027 negotiations |

The scenario effects are our assessment, drawn from the market analysis in the sources.

## Questions to ask copper-intensive suppliers

1. Is your price indexed to COMEX or LME, and what premium is built in?
2. How much of your copper is domestic or already in U.S. inventory, and how much would face a refined-copper tariff?
3. If a tariff is announced, will the surcharge apply only to imported cathode, and how will you show it?
4. How will price adjustments reverse if the tariff is delayed or dropped?
5. Are any of our parts classified as semi-finished copper products already paying the 50% duty?

## What procurement teams should do

- **Don't pre-pay a tariff.** Reject 2027 price increases that assume a refined-copper duty until one is announced; add a clause that applies a tariff surcharge only if and when it takes effect.
- **Re-check the index.** Where contracts reference COMEX, the narrowing premium should be showing up in lower prices relative to LME; ask suppliers to show it.
- **Split copper from conversion cost.** For wire, cable and motor windings, ask for metal and fabrication priced separately so metal moves with the index and conversion stays fixed.
- **Review semi-finished classifications.** Confirm which imported parts already pay the 50% Section 232 duty and whether domestic alternatives now price below landed cost.
- **Set a decision trigger.** Agree with key suppliers now what happens to prices within 30 days of a tariff announcement, either way.

## What to watch next

- **Oct. 19–23: LME Week in London**, with the LME Metals Seminar on Oct. 19. Watch for producers' 2027 premium offers.
- **A Commerce or White House decision on refined copper.** Any announcement before Jan. 1 would reset U.S. premiums quickly.
- **COMEX inventory direction.** Falling U.S. warehouse stocks would confirm metal is moving back out as the arbitrage closes.
