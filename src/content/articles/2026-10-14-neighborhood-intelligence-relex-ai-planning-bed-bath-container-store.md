## Why it matters

Retailers are replacing separate forecasting, replenishment and financial planning tools with one AI-driven system. For their procurement teams, that means purchase orders driven by a single demand signal. For their suppliers, it means more frequent, more precise orders and less room for missed lead times.

## The details

Neighborhood Intelligence, the holding company for Bed Bath & Beyond and The Container Store, will run forecasting, replenishment and allocation on Relex Solutions' platform, according to an Oct. 8 Relex announcement. Merchandise financial planning, open-to-buy and diagnostics will move to the same system, giving planners and buyers one view of the business across more than 335 stores and several online brands in North America.

The company expects better in-stock rates, improved sell-through and faster decisions. Ashley Prado, COO of the retail pillar at Neighborhood Intelligence, said the company needed a platform that could handle allocation for a business growing quickly in scale and complexity.

The vendor's retail customer list has been growing. Dollar General said in August it would use the platform for store replenishment, supplier coordination and ordering. Lowe's connected its in-house supply chain technology to Relex forecasting, replenishment and allocation. Guitar Center, United Natural Foods and Wawa also use it.

## What procurement teams should do

- **If you're the buyer:** Make sure supplier lead times, minimum order quantities and capacity limits in the planning system are current. AI replenishment is only as good as the constraints it plans around.
- **If you're a supplier to these retailers:** Expect more frequent orders and tighter fill-rate scorecards. Ask for forecast sharing and agree on how forecast changes inside your lead time are handled.
- **Align open-to-buy with sourcing.** When financial plans and replenishment sit in one system, sourcing events and supplier commitments need to feed it on the same calendar.
- **Measure what changes.** Track forecast accuracy, in-stock rate and expedite costs before and after rollout, so the business case is tested, not assumed.

## What to watch next

- **Rollout timing.** Watch how quickly forecasting and allocation go live across 335-plus stores.
- **Supplier collaboration.** Retailers on shared planning platforms increasingly expect suppliers to work from the same forecasts.
- **Results.** In-stock and sell-through improvements will show whether consolidating planning on one platform pays off.

## The bigger picture

Planning consolidation is one of the most practical AI use cases in retail supply chains because the gains show up in inventory and availability, not just analyst time. For procurement, the shift moves leverage toward suppliers that can plan with the customer's data and away from suppliers that compete on price alone.
