## How the electrical equipment market works

Electrical equipment covers a wide range: motors and drives, transformers, switchgear, circuit breakers, generators, wiring devices and controls. Much of it is engineered or built to order, especially larger items, so lead times depend on factory slots rather than stock. Prices reflect metals (copper, steel and aluminum), electronic components and labor.

The biggest items, power transformers and medium-voltage switchgear, are made by a small number of manufacturers, and their key materials have few suppliers. Grain-oriented electrical steel, the core material in transformers, comes from only one U.S. producer, Cleveland-Cliffs' Butler Works.

## What is driving prices now

**Demand.** Utilities, data center builders and manufacturers are all expanding power capacity. The electrical equipment industry led ISM's list of growing industries in September.

**Materials.** Steel, copper and aluminum are all elevated, and copper is at a 12-month high. Producer prices for electrical machinery and equipment were up 13.5% on the year through August, according to our [Cost Pressure Monitor](/cost-monitor/electrical/).

**Shortages and lead times.** ISM respondents reported electrical and electronic components in short supply, and capital equipment lead times averaged 176 days in September.

**Trade.** Some metal-intensive grid equipment carries a 15% Section 232 rate through 2027.

## Where the negotiating leverage sits

Suppliers have the edge. Factory slots are scarce and buyers compete for them. Procurement's leverage comes from ordering early, committing to volume across several projects and giving suppliers accurate forecasts.

## Supply risk: what could go wrong

- **Project delays** when a transformer or switchgear lineup arrives months late.
- **Component shortages** that hold up finished equipment.
- **Price escalation** clauses that pass metal increases through after order.
- **Few alternatives** for large, engineered equipment.

## How to buy it now

1. **Plan from the need date backward**, using supplier-quoted lead times, not historical ones.
2. **Reserve factory slots** for 2027 projects now, even before final specifications.
3. **Fix price at order**, or agree a transparent metal adjustment if suppliers insist.
4. **Standardize specifications** across sites so you can move equipment between projects.
5. **Qualify a second source** for critical parts and spares.

## What to watch

- Monthly producer prices and supplier lead time quotes.
- New capacity, including Cleveland-Cliffs' electrical steel expansion, due in early 2028.
- Section 232 changes affecting grid equipment.
