## How corrugated pricing works

Corrugated boxes are made from containerboard, which comes in two parts: linerboard for the flat outer layers and medium for the fluted middle. Integrated producers own both the paper mills and the box plants; independent box makers buy containerboard and convert it. Box prices follow containerboard prices, usually with a lag of one or two months, and most large contracts tie box prices to a published containerboard price.

The industry is concentrated among a handful of large integrated producers in North America, which gives them influence over when price increases are announced and how well they stick. Buyers typically see increases arrive as letters announcing a containerboard increase, followed by box price changes under their contract formula.

## What is driving prices now

**Acceleration from a low base.** U.S. producer prices for converted paper and paperboard, which include corrugated boxes, were up only 4.4% on the year through August, but the three-month pace accelerated to 12.9% annualized and prices were at their 12-month high, according to our [Cost Pressure Monitor](/cost-monitor/paper/).

**Packaging demand.** ISM respondents listed packaging among commodities rising in price in September, and rising manufacturing orders and restocking support box demand.

**Costs.** Energy, chemicals, freight and recovered fiber all feed into mill costs.

## Where the negotiating leverage sits

Balanced, moving toward suppliers. Supply is available, but the trend has turned up. The window to lock terms is before your next contract reset.

## Supply risk: what could go wrong

Risk is low. The main risks are mill outages or closures, regional freight disruptions and price increases that land between contract resets.

## How to buy it now

1. **Check reset dates** in your corrugated contracts and get ahead of them.
2. **Tie changes to a published index** with a lag and a trigger band.
3. **Right-size and standardize boxes** to cut board use and freight.
4. **Consolidate volume** with fewer plants where service allows.
5. **Review board grades** against actual performance needs.

## What to watch

- Containerboard price announcements.
- Monthly producer price data for paper and paperboard.
- Manufacturing and retail demand into the holiday season.
