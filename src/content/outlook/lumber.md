## How the wood market works

Lumber is a classic commodity: sawmills sell dimensional lumber and panels at market prices that can swing widely with housing demand, weather, wildfires and mill closures. Most procurement teams buy wood indirectly, as pallets, crates, packaging, furniture, cabinetry and fixtures, so the effect arrives through supplier price changes rather than lumber quotes.

The U.S. imports a significant share of its softwood lumber, mainly from Canada, and a large share of furniture and cabinetry from Asia. That makes trade measures a big part of the category's cost.

## What is driving prices now

**Tariffs.** Section 232 duties on wood products took effect Oct. 14, 2025: about 10% on certain softwood timber and lumber, and 25% on certain upholstered wooden furniture and kitchen cabinets and vanities. Increases to 30% and 50% were delayed by a year and are now scheduled for Jan. 1, 2027, as our [tariff coverage](/articles/2026-10-09-wood-tariffs-kitchen-cabinets-vanities-50-percent-upholstered-furniture-january-2027/) explains.

**Prices.** Lumber producer prices were up 7.6% on the year through August, according to our [Cost Pressure Monitor](/cost-monitor/lumber/).

**Regulation abroad.** EU deforestation rules take effect Dec. 30, 2026 for wood and wood products sold into the EU.

## Where the negotiating leverage sits

Balanced. Lumber is not short, so pallet and packaging buyers have options. For imported cabinets and furniture, the Jan. 1 increase shifts leverage toward domestic suppliers, whose quotes become more competitive.

## Supply risk: what could go wrong

- **Tariff step-ups** on Jan. 1, or changes through country agreements.
- **Wildfires and mill closures** that tighten regional supply.
- **Documentation gaps** for EU-bound wood under the deforestation rules.

## How to buy it now

1. **Check pallet and crating contracts** for how lumber changes pass through.
2. **Classify furniture and cabinetry** against the proclamation to confirm which rate applies.
3. **Decide on pull-forward imports** before Jan. 1 using a holding-cost break-even.
4. **Re-quote domestic suppliers** for 2027 cabinet and furniture projects.
5. **Collect origin data** for wood sold into the EU.

## What to watch

- Country agreements or another delay before Jan. 1.
- Monthly lumber prices.
- EU guidance on the deforestation rules.
