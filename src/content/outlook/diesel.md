## How diesel prices are set

The retail diesel price is built from four pieces: the cost of crude oil, the refining margin to turn it into diesel, distribution and retail costs, and taxes. Crude gets most of the attention, but this year the refining margin has done much of the work. Diesel is a "middle distillate," competing for refinery output with jet fuel and heating oil, so when refineries are damaged or shipping routes close, diesel tightens faster than crude.

For procurement teams, diesel matters twice. It is a direct purchase for fleets and equipment, and it is embedded in almost every freight bill through fuel surcharges. Using ATRI's 2025 cost data, fuel is roughly a fifth of a truck's operating cost, by our calculation.

## What is driving prices now

**The war with Iran.** Ship crossings through the Strait of Hormuz, which normally carries about 20% of global seaborne oil and LNG, are down more than 80% since Feb. 28, according to S&P Global. Mediterranean diesel cargoes were about 70% above their pre-war level in late August, and European diesel refining margins are the highest in at least 15 years.

**Russia.** Drone strikes have taken roughly half of Russia's refining capacity offline, according to S&P Global Energy CERA. The October deal announced by the White House is unlikely to add meaningful volume, and Congress banned Russian energy imports in 2022.

**Seasonality.** Heating oil demand rises from November, which typically supports diesel prices.

**Outlook.** Morgan Stanley expects the oil market to stay in deficit through the first quarter of 2027.

## Where the negotiating leverage sits

Suppliers hold it, in the sense that no buyer can negotiate the price of fuel itself. Procurement's leverage is in contract structure: the surcharge formula, the base price, the fuel economy assumption and audit rights. Two carriers can bill very different fuel on the same diesel price, as our [diesel analysis](/articles/2026-10-14-diesel-record-russia-deal-red-dye-order-fuel-surcharges/) shows.

## Supply risk: what could go wrong

Physical shortages are rare in the U.S., but price spikes are not. An escalation in the Gulf, a refinery outage or a cold winter can move diesel by a dollar a gallon within weeks. In March, Dubai crude spiked to $169.75 a barrel when Hormuz traffic nearly stopped.

## How to manage it

1. **Index every surcharge** to the weekly DOE/EIA diesel price with a published table.
2. **Normalize base price and fuel economy** across carriers in your RFPs so bids are comparable.
3. **Audit invoices** against the table each month.
4. **Budget on current prices** with a volatility band, not on a return to 2025 levels.
5. **Reduce fuel exposure**: consolidate loads, raise trailer utilization and test intermodal on long lanes.

## What to watch

- Weekly EIA diesel prices.
- Strait of Hormuz traffic and any ceasefire talks.
- Winter heating demand and distillate inventories.
