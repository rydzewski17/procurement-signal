## How ocean freight is priced

Container shipping is sold by the container, usually quoted per forty-foot equivalent unit (FEU) or twenty-foot equivalent unit (TEU). The total price has a base rate plus surcharges: bunker (fuel), peak season, congestion, canal and terminal charges among them. Shippers buy either through annual or multi-month contracts with carriers, through forwarders who aggregate volume, or on the spot market.

The key difference from most categories is that a contract is not a guarantee of space. Carriers allocate capacity across contract and spot customers, and when ships are full, lower-paying contract cargo can be "rolled" to a later sailing. That is why allocation, roll protection and blank-sailing terms matter as much as the rate.

## What is driving prices now

**Split markets.** Far East–U.S. West Coast spot rates were $8,336 per FEU on Oct. 7, while Far East–North Europe averaged $3,645, down 34% since July, according to Xeneta data reported by FreightWaves.

**Carrier pricing power.** OOCL's third-quarter trans-Pacific revenue rose 71.8% on 13.1% more volume. Revenue growing far faster than volume means price, not demand, is doing the work.

**Reliability.** Only 27% of vessels arrived on time in September, the fourth straight monthly decline, with an average delay of six days, according to Xeneta. Typhoons, port congestion in Asia and bunker costs are the main causes.

**Routing.** The Port of Los Angeles handled a record 1.04 million TEUs in September as disruption on the Suez and Panama routes pushed more cargo to the West Coast.

## Where the negotiating leverage sits

Balanced overall, but very different by lane. On Asia–Europe, falling rates hand leverage to shippers in contract talks now. On the trans-Pacific, carriers still hold it, though the first daily rate decline in weeks came on Oct. 7.

## Supply risk: what could go wrong

- **Delays** of a week or more that force extra safety stock or expedited freight.
- **Blank sailings** that cancel the space you planned on.
- **Rolled cargo** pushed to spot rates.
- **Canal restrictions** at Panama in a dry season, which shift cargo and costs.

## How to buy it now

1. **Segment by lane.** Push for lower fixed rates on Asia–Europe; on the trans-Pacific, use shorter terms or index-linked rates with a ceiling.
2. **Negotiate service, not just price.** Named services, roll protection, blank-sailing notice and reliability reporting.
3. **Split volume** across carriers and alliances.
4. **Update planning lead times** using current delay data.
5. **Benchmark every lane** against spot and long-term indexes before signing.
