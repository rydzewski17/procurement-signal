## How the copper market works

Copper is priced off global exchanges, and most products made from it carry a metal component that moves with that price plus a fabrication charge. Wire and cable makers, for example, usually publish a copper-based price adjustment or quote with a "copper base" that resets with the market. Brass mills price similarly, with a metal value and a conversion charge.

For procurement teams, the important point is where copper hides. Direct purchases of copper rod, tube or sheet are only part of the exposure. Copper sits inside motors, transformers, switchgear, HVAC equipment, cable assemblies and electronics, and suppliers of those products pass metal changes through, often with a delay.

## What is driving prices now

**Demand from electrification.** Grid upgrades, data centers, electric vehicles and electrified equipment all use large amounts of copper, and new mines take many years to develop. That structural gap is why copper has held up even when other metals eased.

**Trade measures.** Copper and copper derivatives are covered by the April 2026 Section 232 metals proclamation, adding a duty layer to imported products.

**Price momentum.** U.S. producer prices for copper and brass mill shapes were at a 12-month high in August, up 20.9% on the year, according to our [Cost Pressure Monitor](/cost-monitor/copper/). ISM respondents also listed copper among commodities rising in price in September.

## Where the negotiating leverage sits

Suppliers have the edge. With prices at a high and demand firm, pass-through requests are coming. Procurement's leverage comes from insisting on transparency: copper weight, reference price and base, so that only the true metal effect passes through and reverses when prices fall.

## Supply risk: what could go wrong

- **Pass-through stacking**, where suppliers add metal surcharges on top of general increases that already included metal.
- **Lead times for copper-heavy equipment** such as transformers and switchgear, which ISM respondents reported in short supply.
- **Theft.** CargoNet recorded metal thefts rising from 54 to 80 incidents year over year in the second quarter, with copper the main target. See our [cargo theft coverage](/articles/2026-08-10-cargo-theft-q2-2026-losses-304-million-metals-technology/).

## How to buy it now

1. **Map copper content.** For your top electrical and mechanical categories, ask suppliers for copper weight per unit.
2. **Agree a metal adjustment formula.** Reference price, base, timing and both-direction adjustment.
3. **Separate metal from general increases.** Don't accept a general increase that already includes metal plus a separate surcharge.
4. **Secure long-lead equipment early.** For transformers and switchgear, order date matters more than a few points of price.
5. **Protect shipments.** Use vetted carriers and tracking for high-value copper loads.

## What to watch

- Monthly producer price data and exchange prices.
- Changes to Section 232 derivative coverage.
- Lead times on copper-intensive electrical equipment.
