## How truckload pricing works

Truckload freight is bought in two ways. Contract rates are agreed per lane, usually in an annual bid, and loads are "tendered" to the contracted carrier through a routing guide. Spot rates are the price for an individual load on the open market, often through a broker. Fuel is normally billed separately through a surcharge tied to the weekly diesel price.

The contract rate only works if carriers accept the loads. When the market tightens, carriers reject tenders and loads fall through the routing guide to backup carriers or the spot market, where they cost more. That makes tender acceptance as important a measure as the rate itself.

## What is driving prices now

**Capacity leaving the market.** SONAR data shows tender rejections up 268% since October 2023 while tender volume rose about 9%, according to FreightWaves. ATRI found carriers cut effective capacity 5.5% in 2025.

**Carrier costs.** The average cost of running a truck reached a record $2.336 per mile in 2025, with tolls, repairs, driver benefits, tires and insurance rising fastest.

**Rates.** The Cass linehaul index rose 10.1% on the year in September, its 21st straight annual increase. SONAR put contract rates at a 52-week high of $2.72 per mile plus fuel on Oct. 9, with spot rates up nearly 50%.

**Consolidation and borders.** C.H. Robinson's planned $5.8 billion acquisition of RXO would give it about 20% of the brokered freight market, and visa restrictions have tightened cross-border capacity with Mexico.

## Where the negotiating leverage sits

Suppliers have the edge, and analysts describe the repricing as only about halfway done. Shippers regain some leverage through what carriers value: consistent volume, fast loading and unloading, quick payment and fair accessorial terms.

## Supply risk: what could go wrong

- **Rejected tenders** pushing freight to the spot market at much higher rates.
- **Regional spikes** from storms, such as the Gulf Coast disruption in early October.
- **Broker integration** disrupting service on accounts that use both C.H. Robinson and RXO.
- **Fraud**, which rises when shippers rush to cover loads with unfamiliar carriers.

## How to buy it now

1. **Segment lanes** by volume and service need before you bid.
2. **Commit volume** on core lanes to asset carriers you trust, with a backup.
3. **Keep fuel separate** through an indexed surcharge.
4. **Track tender acceptance** by carrier every month.
5. **Budget realistically**: Q3 dry van contract rates were already about 16% above the 2025 average, by our calculation.
