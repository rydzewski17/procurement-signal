## How the chemicals market works

Industrial chemicals fall into two broad groups. Commodity chemicals, such as caustic soda, ethylene derivatives and solvents, are made in large volumes, priced off published market assessments and sold by several producers. Specialty chemicals are formulated for a specific use, sold by fewer suppliers and priced on performance rather than a benchmark.

Costs for both start with feedstocks and energy: natural gas, natural gas liquids, crude oil derivatives and electricity. When those move, commodity chemical prices follow quickly; specialty prices follow with a lag and rarely come down as fast as they went up.

## What is driving prices now

**Easing from the peak.** U.S. producer prices for industrial chemicals eased over the three months through August but were still 13.3% above a year earlier, according to our [Cost Pressure Monitor](/cost-monitor/chemicals/).

**Energy and feedstock risk.** The war with Iran has disrupted oil and LNG flows through the Strait of Hormuz, and about 17% of Qatar's LNG export capacity was taken offline in March, according to QatarEnergy. That keeps energy-intensive chemical production costly, especially in Europe and Asia, as our [six-month review](/articles/2026-08-28-us-iran-war-six-months-hormuz-tanker-rates-diesel-jet-fuel/) explains.

## Where the negotiating leverage sits

Procurement has the edge on commodity chemicals, where prices have eased and several producers compete. On specialty chemicals with one qualified source, leverage is closer to balanced. The strongest position is a contract that already passes feedstock changes through both ways.

## Supply risk: what could go wrong

- **Plant outages and force majeure**, which can remove a large share of supply at once.
- **Gulf Coast storms**, which affect many U.S. chemical plants at the same time.
- **Energy shocks** linked to the Middle East.
- **Single-source specialties**, where qualification of a second source takes months.

## How to buy it now

1. **Ask for the cost build.** Before accepting any increase, ask how much is feedstock, energy, freight and conversion.
2. **Use feedstock-indexed formulas** for commodity chemicals, with adjustments in both directions.
3. **Qualify second sources** for critical specialty chemicals.
4. **Review force majeure clauses**, including notice, allocation and mitigation duties.
5. **Hold some safety stock** for products made at only one or two plants.

## What to watch

- Natural gas and oil prices, and Middle East shipping.
- Hurricane season on the Gulf Coast.
- Monthly producer price data for chemicals.
