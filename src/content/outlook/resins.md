## How the resin market works

Resins such as polyethylene, polypropylene, PVC and PET are made from petrochemical feedstocks, mainly ethane and propane in North America and naphtha in much of the rest of the world. Producers sell to converters, who mold or extrude them into packaging, films, containers and parts. Converters usually price resin as a pass-through using a published market index, plus a conversion charge for their own work.

That makes resin one of the most transparent categories to manage: if your contract ties the resin share of price to an index, decreases flow to you automatically. If it doesn't, decreases tend to stay with the converter until you ask.

## What is driving prices now

**A summer decline.** U.S. producer prices for plastic resins fell 8.5% over the three months through August and were among the categories that turned lower this year, according to our [Cost Pressure Monitor](/cost-monitor/resins/).

**Feedstock and energy uncertainty.** Resins are tied to oil and gas. The war with Iran has kept refined products and some feedstocks volatile, as our [six-month review](/articles/2026-08-28-us-iran-war-six-months-hormuz-tanker-rates-diesel-jet-fuel/) explains, so declines can reverse.

**Mixed signals from manufacturers.** ISM respondents listed resins among commodities rising in price in September, even as the producer index had been falling. When the index and purchasing managers disagree, it usually means some suppliers are still passing through earlier increases.

## Where the negotiating leverage sits

Procurement has the edge. Supply is available, prices have fallen and converters compete for volume. Buyers who come with index data and a willingness to move volume can win decreases or better terms now.

## Supply risk: what could go wrong

Risk is low. The main risks are a sharp feedstock move tied to the Middle East, weather events that shut Gulf Coast petrochemical plants, and specific grades with fewer producers.

## How to buy it now

1. **Re-bid packaging and molded parts.** The next 60 days are a good window while prices are below their spring levels.
2. **Index the resin share.** Agree the index, the resin weight per unit and how often prices reset.
3. **Ask for decreases on existing contracts** where resin is passed through without an index.
4. **Lock conversion charges** separately, for a longer term.
5. **Keep a feedstock scenario** in your budget in case Middle East disruption reverses the decline.

## What to watch

- Monthly producer price data for resins.
- Crude, natural gas liquids and Middle East shipping news.
- Hurricane season effects on Gulf Coast plants.
